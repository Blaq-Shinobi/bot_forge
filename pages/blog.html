Market bots: information vs. financial advice

It's easy for a bot that reports live prices to start feeling like it's telling you what to do - especially once you add a command called /signals. It isn't, and it's worth being deliberate about that line.

A market-data bot can tell you what's happening: the price right now, how sentiment has shifted, which coins moved the most today. What it can't do is know your finances, your risk tolerance, or your goals - which is exactly what real financial advice requires. Commentary like "watching resistance" is a description of a chart pattern, not a recommendation.

If you're building or deploying a bot like this, say so plainly in the bot itself, not just in a Terms of Service page nobody reads. A one-line disclaimer in your welcome message costs you nothing and means a user reading your bot's output at 2am knows exactly what they're looking at: information to think with, not a signal to act on.

5 Telegram bot ideas beyond customer support

Most people's first bot idea is FAQ auto-replies, and that's a great start - but the same building blocks (commands, keyword replies, scheduled messages) cover a lot more ground:

  • Daily reminders - a scheduled message at a fixed time, for habits, medication, or standups.
  • Community welcomes - an auto-reply that fires on "hi" or "join" to greet new members with house rules.
  • Event RSVPs - commands like /rsvp paired with a scheduled reminder the morning of.
  • Market or price updates - see CryptoPulseBot in Templates for a live example.
  • Feedback collection - a fallback message that doubles as an open invitation: "Type anything and it'll reach the team."

None of these need custom code - just a different combination of the same commands, auto-replies, and schedules already in the Builder.
